# Pricing models

Read this when choosing how to price, or when the user asks "fixed or hourly?".

## Inputs you need from the user

- Hourly or day rate, and currency. Never guess these.
- Whether they charge VAT or sales tax, and the rate.
- Their minimum project size, if they have one.
- Any budget the client mentioned.

## Fixed price

`price = (sum of PERT expected hours × rate) × (1 + buffer%)`, rounded to a clean number.

Use when scope is clear and the client wants budget certainty. Protect it with:

- Named exclusions.
- A fixed number of revision rounds.
- A written change-request process.
- A payment schedule with a deposit.

## Milestones

Split a fixed price into phases. Each milestone has a deliverable, an acceptance rule ("approved in writing, or no feedback within 5 business days"), and a payment.

A common split for a 3-phase project: 40% deposit, 30% at design approval, 30% before launch. For 4+ phases: 30% deposit, then even splits, final 10 to 20% at handover.

## Day or hourly rate

`day rate ≈ hourly rate × billable hours per day` (often 6 to 7, not 8).

Use when scope is unclear or client-led. Always add:

- An estimate range from the PERT numbers.
- A cap: "We will not exceed X hours without your written approval."
- A reporting rhythm (weekly hours summary).

## Retainer

`monthly fee = included hours × rate × (1 − discount%)`. A small discount (0 to 10%) pays for predictability.

Define: included hours per month, what happens to unused hours (expire or roll over one month), response time, extra hours rate, minimum term and notice period.

## Value-based

Anchor to the outcome, not hours. Ask about the value: "If this works, what is it worth to you in the first year?"

A common heuristic: price at 10 to 20% of the first-year value the client describes, then sanity-check against the hourly cost so you never go below it. Only use when the client talks in outcomes and you can influence the result.

## Presenting options

Offer two or three packages built from the same task list:

| | Essential | Standard | Extended |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | Core deliverables | + the features they asked for | + nice-to-haves and support |
| Revisions | 1 round | 2 rounds | 3 rounds |
| Price | lowest | anchor | highest |

Put the one you recommend in the middle and say why. Keep the difference between packages in scope, not in quality.

## Minimums and rush fees

- A minimum project fee protects small jobs from overhead. Ask if the user has one.
- Rush work (deadline shorter than the normal plan) is commonly 25 to 50% extra. Offer it as an option, not a default.

## Discounts

Never discount the rate silently. If the client pushes on price, remove scope instead, or trade a discount for something concrete (larger deposit, longer retainer, case-study rights).
